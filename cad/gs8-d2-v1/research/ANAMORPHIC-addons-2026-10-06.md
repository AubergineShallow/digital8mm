# D2 add-on anamorphic adapters for M12 lenses (research, 2026-10-06)

Scope: afocal, front-mounted anamorphic adapters on an M12 lens (in an M12-to-CS adapter) on the Pi Global Shutter Camera (IMX296, 1456 x 1088, 5.02 x 3.75 mm, crop 6.90).
This file was written from web research only: nothing was bought, mounted or measured. `[unconfirmed]` = found in one weak source, or inferred.
`[est]` = my own geometric estimate. Prices use 1.28 SGD/USD.

## 2. Compatibility on the IMX296

Field of view, and the scene field after the adapter (tan theta_scene = squeeze x tan theta_lens). Entrance pupil (EP) is given at f/2:

| M12 f | 35 mm equiv. | HFOV | DFOV | EP at f/2 | scene HFOV with 1.33x | with 1.55x |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 mm | 27.6 mm | 64.2 deg | 76.2 deg | 2.0 mm | 79.8 deg | 88.4 deg |
| 5 mm | 34.5 mm | 53.3 deg | 64.2 deg | 2.5 mm | 67.6 deg | 75.6 deg |
| 6 mm | 41.4 mm | 45.4 deg | 55.2 deg | 3.0 mm | 58.3 deg | 65.9 deg |
| 8 mm | 55.2 mm | 34.8 deg | 42.8 deg | 4.0 mm | 45.4 deg | 51.9 deg |
| 12 mm | 82.8 mm | 23.6 deg | 29.3 deg | 6.0 mm | 31.2 deg | 35.9 deg |
| Phone main, 24-26 mm equiv. (4:3) | 24-26 mm | 67-72 deg | 80-84 deg | ~3-4 mm (e.g. f ~6.9 mm at f/1.78 = 3.9 mm) [est] | (design point) | (design point) |

Moment says the T-Series was redesigned (20 % larger mount) for phone cameras **under 26 mm equivalent** (cined.com).
Neewer's 1.55x hits the vignetting edge right at about 24 -> 26 mm equivalent.
**By field angle alone, a 4 mm lens (27.6 mm equiv.) is right at the edge of what these adapters are designed for, while 5-12 mm lenses sit comfortably inside it.**

**Vignetting mechanism.** The beam diameter at the adapter's rear glass is roughly:

D = EP + 2 * z * tan(DFOV/2)

where z is the distance from the taking lens' *entrance pupil* (not its front glass) to the adapter's rear clear aperture.
- **Phone:** the stop sits near the front of the lens, so z is ~3-5 mm. That gives D ~ 3.9 + 8 * 0.90 ~ **11 mm**. So these adapters must have a rear clear aperture of at least ~11-12 mm. Makers publish no number; measure it.
- **M12 lens:** the stop is often mid-barrel, so the pupil sits several mm behind the front vertex, and z grows by the pupil depth plus the air gap. With z = 7 mm [est]:
  - 4 mm f/2: D ~ 13 mm (over budget)
  - 6 mm: ~10.3 mm
  - 8 mm: ~9.5 mm
  - 12 mm: ~9.7 mm
- With z = 3 mm (pupil near the front, 1 mm gap), 4 mm f/2 gives D ~ 6.7 mm, which is fine.

Conclusion:
- **6-12 mm M12 lenses should be vignetting-free with any 1.33x phone adapter.**
- 4 mm works only if its pupil is shallow and the gap is at most ~1 mm.
- 1.55x adapters spread the beam more and already vignette on some phones: plan on **6 mm and longer**.
- Upper limit: the Moment M-Series works on phone tele cameras (35mmc), so 12-16 mm f/2 (EP 6-8 mm) should still pass [est].

**How close.** Aim for **0.5-2 mm between the adapter's rear glass and the M12 front vertex**, roughly what a phone case gives.
M12 barrels have a front lip or retaining ring, and some wide-angle lenses have a domed front element: keep at least 0.5 mm clearance.
Mount the adapter on the **fixed housing / front plate, not on the M12 barrel**. The barrel rotates when it is focused, which would also rotate the cylinder axis.
Focus travel is tiny, so a fixed gap works: infinity to 0.3 m is 36/294 ~ 0.12 mm for 6 mm and 64/292 ~ 0.22 mm for 8 mm.
Add a **light-tight sleeve or skirt** over the gap; stray side light in an open gap is the main flare and contrast risk.

**Rotation.** The cylinder axis must be parallel to the sensor rows, within ~0.5-1 deg [est]. If it is off, the image shears and the flares tilt.
- A Moment bayonet indexes the angle repeatably.
- 17 mm threads stop at an arbitrary angle: you need a lock ring. Ulanzi has an adjustment ring; Freewell has a built-in level.
- Check it by shooting a grid or a circle: after desqueeze, the circle must be round with an untilted axis.

**Mounting a Moment without a phone case:**
- (a) Moment **SmartAlign stick-on lens mount**: a T-Series bayonet on a removable adhesive base. Bond it to a printed D2 front plate.
- (b) Moment **Universal Lens Mount (T-Series)**: an 88 g clamp for objects under 14.7 mm thick. Too heavy and bulky.
- (c) Community 3D-printed bayonets:
  - Printables 630536, "Moment bayonet mount for iPhone 15 Pro Max" (press-fit);
  - MakerWorld 143729, an M-Series stick-on mount for the Pixel 7 (its author notes it needs VHB tape);
  - Printables 124858, a Moment anamorphic filter adapter (OpenSCAD).
  These pages returned 403, so I could not verify them.
- (d) Moondog 37 mm thread, or 17 mm-thread metal backplates (SmallRig / Neewer / Tilta), which are easier to bond than a printed thread.

**No Raspberry Pi- or M12-specific anamorphic mount design was found.** D2 would be first-of-kind.

## 3. Output aspect ratios and desqueeze

| Squeeze | AR from 4:3 | Horizontal desqueeze | Vertical desqueeze | 1024 x 768 EVF preview |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.2x | 1.60:1 | 1747 x 1088 | 1456 x 910 | 1024 x 640 |
| 1.33x (4/3) | **1.78:1 = 16:9** | 1941 x 1088 | 1456 x 818 (or 816) | 1024 x 576 (96 px bars top and bottom) |
| 1.55x | 2.07:1 | 2257 x 1088 | 1456 x 702 | 1024 x 494 |
| 2x | 2.67:1 | 2912 x 1088 | 1456 x 544 | 1024 x 384 |

Note that **1.33x on a 4:3 sensor gives 16:9, not the 2.40:1 the phone marketing quotes** (that figure is 1.33x on 16:9 video).
For 2.39:1 you either crop:
- 1.33x: use 812 of 1088 rows, giving 1941 x 812;
- 1.55x: use 944 rows;

or you need ~1.79x.

**Camera pipeline:**
- rpicam-apps documents no anamorphic, desqueeze or pixel-aspect option. I searched the camera_software docs but cannot fully prove the option is absent. Its preview keeps the image's aspect ratio (letterbox).
- libcamera's Raspberry Pi ISP *does* scale non-uniformly when an output stream's aspect ratio differs from ScalerCrop. The Aug 2024 rpi::ScalerCrops patch thread describes a 640 x 640 stream coming out "squished" next to a 16:9 main stream (lists.libcamera.org/pipermail/libcamera-devel/2024-August/043954.html).
- **D2 recipe:**
  1. Set ScalerCrop to the full 1456 x 1088 sensor.
  2. Record the main stream squeezed at 1456 x 1088.
  3. Request the lores / preview stream at 1024 x 576 (1.33x) or 1024 x 494 (1.55x). That stream comes out desqueezed for free, ready to letterbox on the OLED.
- On Pi 5 (PiSP) with per-stream crops, the defaults may preserve aspect by cropping instead. Set the crops explicitly and check [unconfirmed]. Fallback: scale in the EVF compositor (GL texture or DRM plane).

**Files:** keep the squeezed 1456 x 1088 and flag it rather than resampling:
- `ffmpeg -i in.h264 -c copy -bsf:v h264_metadata=sample_aspect_ratio=4/3 out.mp4` changes metadata only, no re-encode.
- Hard desqueeze: `-vf scale=1942:1088,setsar=1`.
- DaVinci Resolve: Clip Attributes -> Pixel Aspect Ratio offers "1.33x Anamorphic", "1.5x" and "Cinemascope (2x)". 1.55x needs a custom scale.

## 4. Practical issues

- **Weight / load path.** Phone adapters weigh 19-49 g (17 mm-thread "pro" models 113-192 g). D2 wants ~200 g at the front anyway, so the mass is welcome. But it must load the **housing front plate, not the plastic M12 / CS holder on the sensor PCB**. A 40 g adapter on a 30 mm lever over the PCB mount risks shifting the sensor tilt and focus.
- **Dust / flare.** The adapter adds 4-6 air-glass surfaces plus the gap. Seal the gap, and use a hood or the front filter thread:
  - Moment: 67 mm clamp
  - ShiftCam: 53 mm
  - Freewell: 58 mm
  - Moondog: 52 mm mount

  Flare colour is a buying choice:
  - Moment: PanaBlue / Gold42 / Natural
  - Freewell: Blue / Gold / Neutral
  - Sirui, Neewer: blue
  - Moondog: purple
  - Kowa B&H: amber
- **Min. focus.** Afocal adapters are set for infinity. Focusing with the taking lens works down to ~**0.7-0.9 m** (ShiftCam spec). Closer than that, the horizontal and vertical focus split and the image softens; reviewers saw this on the Moment M. For closer work add a weak (+0.5 to +1 dioptre) spherical close-up lens in front of the adapter.
- **Edges.** Moment admits 1.55x is softer at the margins. Distortion is specified as -18 % (1.33x) and -37 % (1.55x) across the full phone field. On a 6-8 mm lens the narrower field should show much less [est]. The IMX296's 1.6 MP is forgiving.
- **Astigmatism / aperture.** With a 2-4 mm pupil, cylindrical astigmatism stays small, but MFT users stop the Moondog to ~f/5.6 and Optex/Century advise stopping 1-2 stops. **Most M12 lenses have a fixed aperture**, so pick an f/2.4-2.8 lens (or one with an iris) rather than f/1.4-1.6. Expect to re-tune the lens-shading calibration (ALSC) in the IMX296 tuning file, because the adapter adds corner fall-off [est].

## 5. Ranked shortlist for D2

1. **Moment 1.33x Mobile Lens II (T-Series), ~$150 / S$192.** Best optics (3 elements, three flare options), indexed bayonet with a stick-on mount, and the larger T-Series rear aperture tolerates the M12's deeper pupil best. Expected vignetting-free: **~5-12 mm (4 mm marginal)** [est].
2. **Ulanzi 1.33x Pro, 3rd gen, 31.5 g, 17 mm thread, ~$60-80 / ~S$77-102 [unconfirmed price].** The cheapest, lightest way to prove the geometry; a 17 mm metal backplate is easy to bond. Expected **~5-12 mm** [est]; check how the rotation lock holds.
3. **ShiftCam LensUltra 1.33x, 34 g, ~$135 / S$173.** The only one with a published min. focus (70-90 cm) and a real 53 mm filter thread; needs the S.Mount or a printed copy. Expected **~5-12 mm** [est].
4. **Moment 1.55x (T-Series), $125-199 / S$160-255.** For a wider 2.07:1 look without cropping, at the cost of soft and vignetting-prone margins. Expected **~6-12 mm** [est].

Alternates:
- Sirui VD-01, the lightest at 19 g, if it can still be found.
- A used Moment M-Series 1.33x (~$50) as the cheapest Moment-bayonet test.
- A Moondog 37 mm, if a used one appears.

## Verdict

An add-on anamorphic on an M12 lens is **realistic on D2**, and better matched than most camera-lens setups. An IMX296 with a 5-8 mm M12 lens has a field the same as or narrower than theirs (35-55 mm equivalent against their 24-26 mm) and the same 2-4 mm entrance-pupil class as the phone main cameras these adapters are designed for. So the optics should pass the field without vignetting, provided the adapter's rear glass sits within ~1-2 mm of the M12 front.

The real work is mechanical and in software:
- a housing-mounted, light-sealed, rotation-indexed adapter seat that never loads the sensor-PCB holder;
- a fixed-aperture M12 lens of about f/2.4-2.8;
- the ISP "full-sensor ScalerCrop + 1024 x 576 lores" trick for a desqueezed EVF;
- a SAR = 4/3 flag on recorded files.

Expect 1.33x to give 16:9 (crop for 2.39:1), and to focus no closer than ~0.8 m without a dioptre.

**Prototype first:** a cheap 17 mm-thread 1.33x (Ulanzi 3rd gen) on a printed front plate with a bonded 17 mm backplate.
- Test it on 4, 6 and 8 mm M12 lenses against a white wall (vignetting) and a grid (rotation and astigmatism).
- If 6 mm is clean, buy the Moment 1.33x T-Series II and a SmartAlign stick-on mount for the release version.
