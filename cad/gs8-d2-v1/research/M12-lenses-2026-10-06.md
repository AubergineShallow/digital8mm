# D2 r4: M12 (S-mount) lenses on the Raspberry Pi Global Shutter camera

Research date: 2026-10-06. Web sources only; nothing was bought or measured. **[unconfirmed]** marks facts no primary source states.
Crop factor 6.90 (43.27 / 6.27). IMX296 active area 5.02 x 3.75 mm. Horizontal FOV = 2·atan(2.51/f).

## 1. Adapters, and whether infinity focus is possible

**The mount.** The GS camera "has the same C/CS-mount lens assembly as the Raspberry Pi High Quality Camera". Back focus is 12.5-22.4 mm.
The IR-cut filter is built in, and it can be removed but not refitted [GS brief p.2-3](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/gs-camera/gs-camera-product-brief.pdf).
Raspberry Pi says plainly that "the GS Camera is available only with a C/CS-mount". There is no M12 GS variant.
The filter is a Hoya CM500 about 1.1 mm thick [RPi camera docs](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/camera.html). It sits on the back of the mount assembly, just above the sensor (already recorded in `research/ZOOM-machine-vision-2026-10-06.md`).
The GS drawing (brief p.4) looks into the throat and shows two things below the 1"-32 thread:
- a **dia 22.4 bore**;
- an **8.5 mm square window** at the bottom of the mount (the filter aperture).

An M12 barrel is 12 mm across, wider than the 8.5 mm window. So the barrel can go no deeper than the floor of that window. **No source gives the depth of that floor or of the CS thread. It is the deciding bench measurement (section 5).**

**The HQ M12 variant (SC0870)** has its own milled M12 mount. Lens back focus on it is 2.6-11.8 mm, and it ships with three lock rings [HQ brief](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/hq-camera/hq-camera-product-brief.pdf), [PiShop](https://www.pishop.us/product/raspberry-pi-hq-camera-m12/), [Farnell SC0870](https://cpc.farnell.com/raspberry-pi/sc0870/raspberry-pi-hq-camera-m12-mount/dp/SC19769).
- The 2.6 mm minimum suggests an M12 lens rear can sit about 2.6 mm from the sensor with the filter in place [inference].
- The M12 mount is not sold separately [unconfirmed].
- Entaniya sells a third-party M12 mount that replaces the HQ's C/CS mount. You move the IR filter across and reuse the two screws. Prices: RP-HQ-M12-KIT JPY 5,900 (parts), RP-HQ-M12 JPY 15,900 (assembled) [Entaniya](https://e-products.entaniya.co.jp/en/list/raspberry-pi/interchangeable-lens-camera-module/lens-adapter-for-raspberrypi-hq-camera/raspberry-pi-hq-camera-m12-lens-mount-adapter).
- Because the lens assembly is shared, it should fit the GS [unconfirmed for GS]. But it removes the dia 36 back-focus ring that the D2 chassis bore is built around.

**Geometry on the GS (with the ring at minimum, CS flange 12.5 mm from the sensor):**
- With CLA-M12-CS fully seated, its M12 thread spans roughly 3.6-8.6 mm above the sensor [derived from drawing].
- The lens rear sits at about MBFL + 0.4 mm. The extra ~0.4 mm is focus shift from the 1.1 mm CM500 glass, t(1-1/n).
- Lenses with MBFL ~3-7 mm engage the thread. The GS back-focus ring lifts the whole adapter and adds thread overlap for longer-MBFL lenses.
- With the thin CLA127, the M12 thread sits about 9-12.5 mm up, or lower if screwed deeper. Any lens with ≥10 mm of thread engages.

**The risk is the low end.** Lenses with MBFL ≤ ~2.5-3 mm (CIL043, CIL125 below) may hit the filter window floor before reaching infinity.

**Field reports:**
- No forum report was found of an M12 lens used **on the GS camera**.
- On the HQ, which has the same mount, it works:
  - Arducam sells HQ M12 kits tested on that camera, e.g. LK003 at EUR 109 with 6 lenses and an adapter [Welectron](https://welectron.com/Arducam-LK003-M12-Lens-Kit-for-Raspberry-Pi-High-Quality-IMX477-Camera_1).
  - A forum user fixed a no-focus problem by dropping the C-CS spacer and replacing a bad lens [RPi forum t=279395](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=279395).
  - Some HQ rings take Arducam adapters only half a turn (a thread-tolerance issue). The workaround is to set focus with the lens thread [Arducam forum](https://forum.arducam.com/t/m12-adapter-for-180-lens-not-threading-to-hq-backfocus-ring/1061).
- In a GS thread, an experienced user suggests M12 + adapter as the shortest option, warning "It won't be as easy to focus" [RPi forum t=382714](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=382714).

**Native M12 IMX296 boards** (noted only; D2 uses the RPi GS). Same driver, no adapter, and far lighter than the GS mount:
- Arducam B0445: colour, 6 mm f/2.0 M2506ZH04 lens, 13 mm holder, 38x38 board, EUR 74.90 [Welectron](https://welectron.com/Arducam-B0445-158MP-IMX296-Color-Global-Shutter-Camera-Module-with-M12-Lens-for-Raspberry-Pi). A mono twin exists.
- Leopard LI-IMX296-MIPI-M12: about 12 g, 4.2 mm f/2.5 lens with -1.2 % distortion [datasheet](https://leopardimaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/LI-IMX296-MIPI-M12_V1.0.pdf).
- Waveshare IMX296-130 (M12).

## 3. Practical issues

**Focusing.** An M12 lens focuses by turning the whole lens in its 0.5 mm-pitch thread. There is no focus ring, and a lock nut or set screw normally fixes it. The throw is short (Δ ≈ f²/u):
- 6 mm lens: ∞ to 1 m = 0.035 mm (25° of rotation); ∞ to 0.5 m = 50°.
- 8 mm lens: 46° to 1 m; 94° to 0.5 m.
- 12 mm lens: 105° to 1 m.

So a **printed lever collar** clamped to the lens front works like a Nizo-style focus lever, with about 90° of travel covering ∞ to 0.5 m on an 8 mm lens.
- Replace the lock nut with friction plus axial preload: an O-ring or wave washer under a loose nut. Otherwise the thread play (a few hundredths of a mm [unconfirmed]) is about the size of the whole focus throw.
- Turning the lens can shift the image slightly through lens decentre [unconfirmed]; check on the bench.
- **The lens front rotates while focusing.** An anamorphic adapter must therefore mount on the chassis, not on the lens.

**Fixed focus is the cheap option.** Hyperfocal distances, taking the circle of confusion as 2 px (6.9 um):
- 4.4 mm f/3.2: about 0.9 m, so 0.45 m to ∞ is sharp;
- 5.9 mm f/4: about 1.3 m;
- 5.9 mm f/1.7: 3.0 m;
- 8 mm f/2.8: 3.3 m;
- 12 mm f/2: 10 m.

A wide, slow M12 set at hyperfocal behaves like a fixed-focus Super 8 camera.

**No iris.** Every candidate has a fixed aperture; Commonlands sells some at f/2.8-5.6 as separate part numbers. Exposure therefore comes from shutter, gain and ND. In sun at f/2 a 180° shutter at 24 fps needs about ND 7-8 stops (sunny-16 estimate).

**CRA and shading.**
- Sony publishes no IMX296 CRA figure that I could find [unconfirmed]. As a Pregius machine-vision sensor it is expected to be low, around 0-10° [unconfirmed].
- Commonlands puts industrial sensors at "0-15° at image edge". It advises matching lens CRA within ±10° for sensors under 10° [CRA article](https://commonlands.com/blogs/technical/lens-chief-ray-angle-and-mismatch).
- Known or derived lens CRAs:
  - Gaojia 8 mm: ≤ 10.8°;
  - Commonlands CIL052: 12° [page](https://commonlands.com/products/low-distortion-5mm-m12-lens-cil052);
  - Edmund 6 mm: about 11°, from its exit pupil at -9.1 mm;
  - Edmund 8 mm: about 14°, from its exit pupil at -4.1 mm.
- Avoid phone-style short-TTL lenses with CRA of 20-30°: they give colour shading in the corners.
- Remaining falloff can be corrected by re-running ALSC (lens-shading) calibration with Raspberry Pi's camera tuning tool on flat-field captures.

**Quality against the Kowa LM6HC** (6 mm, 1 in format, iris, focus ring, 215 g):
- At 1.58 MP, the all-glass 8-9 element M12s (CIL068, CIL078, CIL085, CIL122) should be close in sharpness [unconfirmed until bench].
- They lose on flare and ghosting control, iris, focus ergonomics and lens-to-lens consistency.
- They save about 190-205 g. Lens plus adapter is 10-20 g, adapter mass [unconfirmed].
- Cost: $19-59 lens plus $6-12 adapter.

## 4. Ranked shortlist for D2

1. **Commonlands CIL068 6.8 mm f/2.5 NIR + CLA127 thin adapter ($55).** 47 mm eq, near the 41 mm default. All glass (8 elements), 12 MP, -3 %. Its long 6.6 mm MBFL keeps the lens well clear of the filter window. EP 7.9 mm.
2. **Commonlands CIL083 8.0 mm f/2.8 NIR + CLA127 ($25).** 55 mm eq, 4 g, -0.5 % distortion. Shallowest EP of the good-circle lenses (5.4 mm) on a dia 14 front, so the best anamorphic candidate. Hybrid glass/plastic.
3. **Commonlands CIL059 5.9 mm f/1.7 (or the f/4 variant) NIR + CLA-M12-CS ($61).** Same 41 mm framing as the Kowa default, and fastest. But its 3.1 mm MBFL needs the floor check, and the deep EP (8-13 mm) behind a dia 20 front makes it poor for anamorphic.
4. **Gaojia/RPi 8 mm f/1.8 12 MP + Arducam UB0225 (about $27).** The Raspberry Pi-recommended lens with published CRA ≤ 10.8° and MBF 6.8. Cheap and fast, but more barrel distortion; EP not published.
5. **Commonlands CIL043 4.4 mm f/3.2 NIR + CLA127 ($45).** 30 mm eq with almost no distortion. Hyperfocal about 0.9 m gives true fixed focus. But MBFL is only 2.4 mm, so it carries the highest risk of hitting the filter window: bench-gate it.

Wildcard: the Marshall CV-2812-3MP varifocal for framing tests only (not parfocal; 35 g).

## 5. Bench checks before committing

1. **Throat depth** on the actual GS, with the ring at minimum. Measure from the CS reference face to (a) the bottom of the 1"-32 thread, (b) the dia 22.4 floor and 8.5 mm window, (c) the top of the filter. Then confirm, for each lens, that MBFL + 0.4 clears the window floor by ≥ 0.3 mm.
2. **Back-focus ring travel.** The 12.5-22.4 mm figure may include the 5 mm C-CS adapter, which would leave about 5 mm of ring travel [unconfirmed]. Measure it.
3. **Infinity focus.** Focus on a target more than 50 m away. Require ≥ 3 turns (1.5 mm) of thread engagement and ≥ 0.5 turn of spare travel beyond infinity. Check that UB0225 and CLA127 thread freely into the ring (the Arducam forum reported tolerance problems).
4. **Vignetting and CRA shading.** Take raw flat-field shots of an opal diffuser over an LED panel. Measure the corner-to-centre ratio per R/G/B channel. Colour drift between channels points to CRA mismatch. Then run ALSC calibration.
5. **Image circle and corners** at infinity and at close focus. The Edmund lenses are marginal.
6. **Focus lever behaviour**: thread play under preload, image shift while rotating, and whether the lever holds under vibration from walking with the camera.
7. **Flare and ghosts** from point lights. Compare NIR and 650 nm IR-cut variants with the CM500 behind them.
8. **Anamorphic fit** (together with the other agent): with the adapter on a chassis mount, check corner vignetting against EP depth and front diameter.

## 6. Verdict

M12 is a sensible **secondary or light-weight path** for D2, not a drop-in replacement for the Kowa.

**For it:**
- It cuts about 200 g off the nose of the camera, which also relieves the plastic GS mount.
- Lens plus adapter costs $25-60.
- Good machine-vision M12s (Commonlands 8-9 element designs, low distortion, CRA around 10-12°) resolve more than the 1.58 MP IMX296 needs.
- A wide, slow M12 set at hyperfocal suits the Nizo idea of a fixed-focus or lever-focus cine camera.

**Against it:**
- No iris, so ND carries all the exposure control.
- Focus means turning the whole lens through a few tenths of a millimetre, which needs a printed lever with preload. Pulling focus during a shot is crude.
- There is no parfocal M12 zoom.
- Whether infinity is reachable depends on one unmeasured number: how deep the GS throat floor and filter window sit.

**For the anamorphic plan:** M12 lenses are better matched to clip-on phone anamorphics than the 215 g Kowa. Their front diameters (14-20 mm) and entrance pupils (1.4-6 mm across) are phone-like. But the pupils sit 5-13 mm behind the front glass, against about 1-2 mm on a phone. So prefer a shallow-pupil, ~8 mm lens (CIL083 or Edmund 8 mm), and mount the anamorphic on the chassis, because the lens rotates as it focuses.

Recommendation: buy CLA127 + CIL068 + CIL083 (about $75) for the bench, run checks 1-4, and keep the Kowa as the release default until they pass.
